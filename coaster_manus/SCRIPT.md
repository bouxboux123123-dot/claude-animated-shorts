# Script and factual note

**Historical and physics grounding:** The story’s specific builder-versus-rival events, hammer-bent loop, test dummy incident, and opening-day climax are not verified by the sources below. Treat them as dramatized fiction. The line “the first ride ever to go upside down” is overbroad if read literally: Guinness World Records documents a full-size centrifugal railway built in 1842, preceding later famous looping coasters.[1] The physics explanation is broadly consistent with roller-coaster loop design: circular loops create higher acceleration around the bottom, while modern profiles use a larger radius at the bottom and tighter curvature near the top.[2]

## Supplied narration

1. He bent the roller coaster's loop out of shape.
2. You see, it's the first ride ever to go upside down,
3. and it cost him everything.
4. So of course, his rival copies it bolt for bolt.
5. But the night before opening,
6. his test dummy comes back crushed like a soda can,
7. but he can't miss the opening,
8. so he grabs a hammer and beats the bottom
9. into a long, lazy curve.
10. Does he know what he's doing? Not really.
11. His rival laughs, then sneaks in,
12. and cranks the sides together until the loop is tall and skinny.
13. So the first cart will snap like a twig.
14. The next morning, the town lines up at the rival's perfect circle,
15. and the rival climbs in front to show off.
16. The owner climbs into his,
17. and as his cart tips over the first hill,
18. he finally sees the tall, skinny loop ahead.
19. But it's too late.
20. You see, a car can creep around a hairpin turn at five,
21. but take it at 60, and it flips.
22. A cart is fastest at the bottom of a loop,
23. and a perfect circle puts a hairpin right there,
24. which is what crushed the dummy.
25. But his hammer made the bottom gentle,
26. and the rival's squeeze moved the hairpin up top,
27. where the cart is crawling.
28. So he floats over without a scratch.
29. Just as the rival's cart hits the bottom of that perfect circle.
