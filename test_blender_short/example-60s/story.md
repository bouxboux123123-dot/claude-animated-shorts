# Short v2: "The Can With No Opener" (~56 s, 212 words)

## Title options
1. Why Soldiers Carried Food They Couldn't Open
2. Why The Can Came 48 Years Before The Can Opener
3. Open The Can: The 48-Year Mistake

## Sources
- Wikipedia, "Can opener" (fetched, checked): first cans heavy, label said "Cut round the top near the outer edge with a chisel and hammer"; Ezra Warner's 1858 sickle-style opener; US Army used it in the Civil War, judged too dangerous for home use. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Can_opener
- Smithsonian Magazine, "Why the can opener wasn't invented until almost 50 years after the can" (confirmed via search snippets only; page returned 403): tin can patented 1810, opener 1858. https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/why-can-opener-wasnt-invented-until-almost-50-years-after-can-180964590/
- Caveat: Robert Yeates patented a claw opener in Britain in 1855, so the narration says "the real can opener arrived in America in 1858" and line 1 is the popular framing (48 years = 1810 to 1858). Dropped the "bayonets/rifle fire" trope (not verified). The 1810 patent was Peter Durand (Britain).

## Narration (212 words)
1. Canned food existed for forty-eight years before anyone invented the can opener.
2. Forty-eight years. Just hammers and bad ideas.
3. Meet Marcel, a very hungry soldier by the river.
4. Today, the army sent him a crate of canned beef.
5. Marcel tries his knife. The knife bends.
6. His boot. The boot hurts more.
7. A rock. The rock loses.
8. Chef Gaston marches over. "Step aside, amateur."
9. He smacks it with a frying pan. Nothing.
10. He sits on it. Still nothing.
11. Marcel hurls it at a tree. The tree loses too.
12. Wait. Who sells food that nobody can open?
13. You see, nobody had thought that far ahead.
14. The can was patented in 1810, in thick iron,
15. often heavier than the food inside.
16. The label's instructions? "Cut round the top with a chisel and hammer."
17. Gaston grabs a chisel and hammer. Marcel closes his eyes. Clang.
18. The lid pops. Marcel takes one bite, and looks betrayed.
19. The first American can opener came in 1858.
20. Soldiers got it in the Civil War.
21. Everyone else? Too dangerous for home use.
22. Marcel, still chewing, calls that progress.

## Cast
- **Private Marcel**: round bean, blue coat, red cap tilted, big brown moustache, tan satchel. Hungry, hopeful, dramatic, loses patience in silence.
- **Chef Gaston**: tall bean, white coat, very tall white hat, thin curled black moustache, apron stained orange. Arrogant expert, never admits defeat, sighs a lot.
- Optional crowd (2-3 bean soldiers, grey-green coats) sit by the barrels in shots 2 and 9 and watch.

## Location and props
One riverside camp: wooden bridge in the distance, trees, reeds, rocks, barrels. Props: a grey iron cube-ish can (cylinder, chunky, with a red band label), wooden crate, knife (flat box), frying pan (disc + stick), chisel (thin box), mallet (cylinder + box), a rock, a small sickle opener (bent box).
